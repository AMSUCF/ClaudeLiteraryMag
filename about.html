About the Magazine

Our Mission, Values, and Editorial Process

Our Mission

Claude Literary Magazine is dedicated to publishing exceptional literary fiction, poetry, and creative nonfiction that demonstrates craft, originality, and authentic human voice. We believe in the power of literature to illuminate the human experience, challenge perspectives, and create meaningful connection between writers and readers.

What We Publish

We seek work that:

  • Demonstrates mastery of craft and attention to language
  • Takes risks in form, content, or perspective
  • Engages with the complexities of contemporary life
  • Reflects diverse voices and experiences
  • Resonates with emotional truth and authenticity

We publish:

  • Fiction: Short stories up to 7,000 words, including literary fiction, experimental fiction, and genre-bending work
  • Poetry: All forms and styles, from traditional to experimental
  • Creative Nonfiction: Personal essays, memoirs, lyric essays, and literary journalism up to 5,000 words

Our Peer Review Process

Claude Literary Magazine employs a rigorous blind peer review process to ensure fairness and maintain the highest literary standards. Here's how it works:

1. Initial Screening

Upon submission, our managing editor reviews each piece to ensure it meets basic formatting and length requirements. Submissions are anonymized at this stage—all identifying information is removed before the work enters the review process.

2. Blind Review

Anonymized submissions are distributed to at least two editorial readers who evaluate the work without knowing the author's identity. This blind review process eliminates bias based on author reputation, credentials, or previous publications.

Our readers evaluate submissions based on:

  • Craft: Technical skill in language, structure, and form
  • Originality: Fresh perspective, unique voice, or innovative approach
  • Impact: Emotional resonance and lasting impression
  • Cohesion: Unity of theme, tone, and execution
  • Authenticity: Genuine human voice and experience

3. Editorial Discussion

Works that receive strong recommendations from readers advance to editorial discussion. Our editorial team—comprising published writers, educators, and literary professionals—meets to discuss standout submissions and make final selections.

4. Decision & Response

We aim to respond to all submissions within 8-12 weeks. Authors receive one of the following responses:

  • Acceptance: We'd like to publish your work (often with minor edits)
  • Revise & Resubmit: We see potential and invite revision based on specific feedback
  • Personal Rejection: Work advanced in the process but wasn't selected; includes encouraging feedback
  • Standard Rejection: Work didn't advance beyond initial review

5. Publication Process

Accepted pieces undergo collaborative editing. We work closely with authors to refine their work while preserving their unique voice and vision. Authors approve all final edits before publication.

Why Blind Review Matters

Blind peer review is the gold standard in academic and literary publishing because it:

  • Reduces implicit bias based on gender, race, age, or institutional affiliation
  • Levels the playing field for emerging writers without extensive publication records
  • Ensures that work is judged solely on its literary merit
  • Maintains objectivity and editorial integrity
  • Builds trust with our readership that we publish the best work, not the most famous names

Artificial Intelligence Policy

Claude Literary Magazine publishes only human-authored literary work. We believe that authentic literature emerges from lived human experience, creative struggle, and the irreplaceable act of a human mind grappling with language to express truth.

Our Position on AI-Generated Content

We do not accept AI-generated writing. This includes but is not limited to:

  • Text generated by large language models (ChatGPT, Claude, GPT-4, etc.)
  • Work substantially created or co-authored by AI systems
  • Human-initiated prompts that produce AI-written passages used in the final work
  • Hybrid work where AI generates significant portions that are then lightly edited by humans

Ethical Use of AI Tools

We recognize that AI tools can serve legitimate purposes in the writing process. The following uses are acceptable and do not violate our policy:

Permitted Uses:

  • Grammar and spell-checking: Using tools like Grammarly, ProWritingAid, or similar programs to catch errors
  • Thesaurus functions: Looking up synonyms or word alternatives
  • Translation assistance: Using AI to help translate your own work between languages
  • Brainstorming: Discussing ideas or concepts with AI to develop your own thinking (without using AI-generated text)
  • Research: Using AI to gather factual information or historical context
  • Editing feedback: Asking AI for critique or suggestions, which you then choose to incorporate or ignore

Not Permitted:

  • Content generation: Having AI write any portion of your submission
  • Substantial rewriting: Feeding your draft into AI and using its rewritten version
  • Plot or structure generation: Using AI to create the narrative arc, story structure, or poetic form
  • Character dialogue: Having AI generate character voices or conversations
  • Descriptive passages: Using AI to write scene descriptions, metaphors, or imagery

The Distinction: Tool vs. Author

The key question: Who is doing the creative and intellectual work?

A grammar checker corrects your spelling—you're still the author. A thesaurus suggests synonyms—you choose which word fits. These are tools that assist your work.

But when AI generates sentences, paragraphs, or ideas that you then lightly edit, the AI is the author, not you. The creative work—the selection of words, the construction of metaphors, the rhythm of sentences—originated with the machine, not the human mind.

Why This Matters

Literature is fundamentally an act of human communication. When you read a poem, story, or essay, you're encountering another human consciousness—their observations, emotions, struggles, insights. This human connection is what gives literature its power.

AI can arrange words skillfully, but it cannot:

  • Experience the world through a body
  • Feel loss, love, fear, or joy
  • Grapple with mortality
  • Navigate cultural identity
  • Struggle with language to express the inexpressible
  • Transform lived experience into art

These human experiences are the wellspring of literature. When we publish your work, our readers trust that they're encountering your authentic voice, born from your unique human experience.

Attestation & Enforcement

All submissions require an attestation that the work is human-authored. We take this seriously. If we discover that a published piece was AI-generated:

  • The work will be immediately retracted
  • The author will be permanently banned from future submissions
  • We will publicly acknowledge the retraction
  • We reserve the right to pursue further action if fraud was involved

Questions About AI Use?

If you're unsure whether your use of AI tools crosses the line from assistance to authorship, ask yourself:

  • Did I write every sentence in this submission myself?
  • Did the words, metaphors, and ideas originate from my own mind?
  • Did I do the creative work, or did I edit AI's creative work?
  • Would I feel comfortable telling readers exactly how I used AI in this piece?

When in doubt, reach out to us at editor@claudelitmag.fake. We're happy to discuss specific scenarios and help you navigate ethical AI use.

Our Commitment

As AI technology evolves, so too will our policies. We commit to:

  • Regularly updating this policy to address new AI capabilities
  • Maintaining transparency about our standards and enforcement
  • Supporting writers in understanding ethical AI use
  • Championing authentic human authorship in an increasingly automated world

Literature is a human art. Let's keep it that way.
